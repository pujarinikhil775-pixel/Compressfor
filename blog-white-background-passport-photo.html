Why a Plain White Background Is Non-Negotiable

According to the U.S. Department of State passport photo requirements, background is one of the specific technical checks every submitted photo goes through. Passport photo processing isn't just reviewed by a person — it runs through automated systems first, specifically checking that your face can be cleanly separated from the background. A shadowed wall, a patterned curtain, or even slightly off-white paint behind you can be enough to fail that check, even if the photo looks perfectly normal to your own eye.

The Core Size Requirements: 2 x 2 Inches

Alongside the background, every US passport photo must be exactly 2 x 2 inches (51 x 51mm), square, in color. Within that frame, your head — measured chin to crown — must fall between 1 inch and 1-3/8 inches (25-35mm), roughly 50-69% of the photo's height. Incorrect head size remains the single most common rejection reason after background issues.

Physical Print vs. Digital Upload — Know the Difference

  • Mailed applications (Form DS-11 or DS-82) — one printed color photo at the full 2 x 2 inch physical size
  • Online renewal (MyTravelGov) — a digital JPEG upload instead, minimum 600 x 600 pixels

Uploading the wrong type for your specific application path is a common technical rejection reason, separate from the background or size being correct.

Background Replacement vs. AI-Edited Photos — What's the Difference?

As of 2026, the State Department has tightened enforcement against AI-edited or filtered photos, using its own detection systems to catch appearance-altering changes — skin smoothing, beautification filters, AI-generated lighting adjustments. Replacing a background to meet the required plain white standard is a routine, expected part of passport photo preparation, not a cosmetic alteration to your face or appearance.

That said, requirements can be interpreted strictly, so it's always worth double-checking your final photo against the current rules at travel.state.gov before submitting, especially close to a deadline.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get a white background for my passport photo online?

What is the exact size of a US passport photo?

2 x 2 inches (51 x 51mm) for printed applications, or a minimum of 600 x 600 pixels in JPEG format for online renewal.

Does replacing my background count as an AI-edited photo?

No. Background replacement to meet the required plain white standard is a routine part of passport photo preparation, not an appearance-altering edit. As of 2026, the AI-edit restrictions target changes to your actual appearance, not the background. Always verify your final photo against current requirements at travel.state.gov before submitting.

Can I wear glasses in a US passport photo?

No, except for documented medical necessity with a signed physician's statement, since 2016.

Can I take a passport photo with my phone?

Yes. A modern phone camera is sharp enough for a passport photo — the main things to get right are lighting, distance, and a plain background, which is exactly what a background replacement tool like this one solves for.

Can I use a selfie for my passport photo?

It's generally better to have someone else take the photo, or use a timer, since a selfie's extended arm angle can distort framing and head position. If a selfie is all you have, keep the phone at eye level and as far away as your arm allows.

Can I smile in a passport photo?

A natural, slight smile is acceptable. A neutral expression is the safest default, but a big open-mouth smile can cause rejection since both eyes and the mouth need to be clearly visible in a natural position.

Can I wear makeup in a passport photo?

Yes, as long as it doesn't significantly alter your natural appearance. Normal everyday makeup is fine; heavy contouring or effects that change your face shape are not.

Can I reuse an old passport photo?

No. Photos must be taken within the last 6 months, even if your appearance hasn't changed.
